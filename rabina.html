Rabina Beauty Parlor

Turning a word-of-mouth salon into a living digital storefront

A family-run beauty parlor and CTEVT-affiliated training center in Kathmandu grew for years on reputation alone — with no way for new clients or students to find it online. I designed and built an 11-page, fully responsive website that captures its warmth and turns browsers into booked appointments.

01 — The Challenge

A beloved salon that nobody could find online

Rabina Beauty Parlor was founded by Sulochana, who built the business one client at a time — a warm smile, consistent quality, and word spreading naturally across the Kathmandu Valley. Over time it grew into a full training center, teaching hundreds of students a year toward self-employment.

But that reputation lived entirely offline. New clients couldn't browse services or prices, brides couldn't picture a package, and prospective students had no way to learn about the CTEVT-affiliated courses. The business was invisible to anyone outside the existing word-of-mouth circle.

The core problem

Two very different audiences — walk-in beauty clients and aspiring students — needed to understand, trust, and act, all from a first visit on a phone.

The design question

How do you translate the feeling of being personally cared for into a website — and make booking or enrolling the obvious next step?

02 — Approach

From goals, to structure, to screens

  1. 1
    Lead with warmth and trustA photo-rich, calm layout and real client voices — the site should feel like the parlor, not a template.
  2. 2
    Make services & prices scannableClear cards, honest “starting at” pricing, and a dedicated bridal journey.
  3. 3
    Give both audiences a clear next step“Book Appointment” for clients; a Training path for students — a CTA on every page.
  4. 4
    Work beautifully on a phone firstMost visitors arrive on mobile, so every layout was designed to collapse gracefully.

Information architecture

I mapped everything into seven top-level destinations so nothing sits more than one tap from home.

03 — Wireframes

Blocking out the homepage before any color

I started low-fidelity to lock in hierarchy: a centered brand, an unmissable nav, a hero that states the promise in one line, and two clear calls to action — before a single photo or pink accent entered the picture.

Decisions made at this stage:

  1. A
    Centered brand lockupThe script logo sits dead-center so the salon's name is the very first thing read.
  2. B
    High-contrast nav barA dark band makes navigation obvious and anchors the airy, light page.
  3. C
    One-line promise + dual CTA“Where Beauty Meets Care” with Book / Explore buttons side by side.
  4. D
    Signature image bandThe row of hands-and-tools shots became the page's memorable visual hook.

05 — Visual System

A palette of ivory, charcoal & rose

The system is deliberately quiet so the work and the photography stand out. A single rose accent — pulled straight from the salon's logo — carries every call to action, while warm ivory and charcoal keep the page calm and premium.

07 — Built with Care

Accessible, fast, and findable

Semantic HTML

Landmark regions, a single H1 per page and a logical heading order — easy for screen readers and crawlers alike.

Keyboard & skip links

A “skip to content” link, visible focus states and ARIA labels keep the whole site navigable without a mouse.

Mobile-first & responsive

Fluid grids and lazy-loaded imagery tested from 375px phones up to wide desktop — no horizontal scroll.

SEO fundamentals

Unique titles, meta descriptions, alt text and a sitemap so a salon that lived offline can finally be searched for.

08 — Outcome & Reflection

From invisible to inviting

The finished site gives Rabina Beauty Parlor something it never had: a front door anyone can walk through. A new client can read the story, scan prices, and book in under a minute; a prospective student can find the training programs that were previously invisible.

What I'd explore next: wiring the appointment form to real scheduling, adding a short bridal gallery with consent-cleared photos, and running a usability pass with first-time visitors to validate the booking flow.
